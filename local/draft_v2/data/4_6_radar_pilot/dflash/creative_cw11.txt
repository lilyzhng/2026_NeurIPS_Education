**Title: The Science of Silence**

The bell rings, and I freeze. My heart is a drum in my chest, thudding so loud I’m sure everyone can hear it. The classroom buzzes with the usual chatter—kids laughing, pencils scratching, the hum of fluorescent lights—but I’m stuck in a bubble, my ears plugged with static. My eyes dart to the front of the room, where Mr. Thompson is handing out project assignments. My fingers twitch at my sides, gripping the edge of my desk like a lifeline.  

*“Pair up with someone you haven’t worked with before,”* he says, his voice muffled by the noise. My stomach drops. *Please don’t be him. Please don’t be him.*  

The paper lands in front of me. My name is scrawled in block letters, and next to it is… *Jared*. My breath catches. My brain short-circuits. I’ve seen him in the hallway, in math class, in the cafeteria—always a few feet away, always just out of reach. He’s the kind of guy who wears his hair in a messy, intentional way, and his smile is like a puzzle piece that fits perfectly in the world, but never in mine.  

I force myself to look up. He’s already scanning the room, his eyes landing on me. My face burns. I want to disappear. My mind races through a dozen rehearsed conversations, each one more desperate than the last.  

**Option One:** Start with a question. *“Hey, Jared. Do you think we should do the ecosystem project together?”* No, that’s too direct. It sounds like I’m begging.  

**Option Two:** Comment on the topic. *“Ecosystems are so cool. I mean, like, how do they even…?”* I trail off, my voice cracking. What if he thinks I’m weird? What if he thinks I’m *too* into it?  

**Option Three:** Pretend to be indifferent. *“Oh, whatever. I guess we’ll have to work together.”* That’s not even a real option. I can’t fake indifference when my pulse is a thousand miles per hour.  

I grip my notebook tighter, the paper crinkling under my fingers. My brain is a tangled web of possibilities, each one worse than the last. What if he says no? What if he laughs? What if he thinks I’m a loser?  

The bell rings again, and the room erupts into motion. I stand, my legs wobbling, and force myself to walk toward him. My feet feel like lead.  

“Hey,” I say, my voice a whisper.  

He looks up, and for a second, the world narrows to his eyes. They’re brown, like the earth after a storm. I want to say something profound, something that will make him remember me. Instead, I blurt out, “So… the ecosystem project. Do you think we should do it together?”  

He blinks. “Yeah, I guess.”  

My chest tightens. *He said “I guess.”* That’s not a yes. That’s not even a *maybe*. I want to apologize, to backtrack, to vanish into the floorboards.  

“I mean, if you’re okay with it,” I add, my voice trembling. “I mean, I’m not trying to—”  

“Hey, it’s fine,” he cuts in, his smile widening. “I’m not bad at this stuff.”  

I freeze. *He’s not bad at this stuff?* Does he think I’m bad? Does he think I’m *bad*? My mind spirals. I want to scream, *I’m not bad! I’m smart! I just… I just can’t talk!*  

“I, uh… I’ll bring the plants,” I say, my voice barely audible.  

“Plants?” He raises an eyebrow.  

“Like… for the ecosystem. You know, the ones that grow in the soil. Or the ones that live in the water. I mean, I’m not sure yet. Maybe we can… discuss it later?”  

He nods, and I’m already backing away, my legs moving on autopilot. My face is hot, my hands are clammy, and my brain is screaming at me to *stop, stop, stop*.  

I sit down at my desk, my notebook open but untouched. My eyes flick to the clock. *Five minutes until the bell rings.* I can’t believe I just did that. I can’t believe I said anything at all.  

*What if he thinks I’m weird?*  

*What if he thinks I’m desperate?*  

*What if he thinks I’m…*  

I close my eyes, my breath shallow. My internal voice is sharp, logical, a thousand words a minute, but my mouth is a desert. I’ve rehearsed this conversation a hundred times in my head, and every time, I’ve imagined it going perfectly. But in reality, I’m a mess. I’m a disaster. I’m…  

*Why do I even care?*  

The thought shocks me. I don’t want to care. I don’t want to be this way. I want to be normal, to be able to talk to people without my brain short-circuiting. I want to be able to look at Jared and not feel like I’m about to combust.  

But I can’t.  

I can’t stop thinking about him. I can’t stop wanting to be near him. I can’t stop… *wanting*.  

My fingers trace the edge of my notebook, my mind drifting to the next time we’ll have to work together. The next time I’ll have to talk to him. The next time I’ll have to… *try again*.  

I know I’ll mess up. I know I’ll say something stupid. I know I’ll feel like a fool.  

But I also know I can’t stop.  

Because even if I’m a disaster, even if I’m weird, even if I’m desperate, I’m still me. And maybe… maybe that’s enough.  

The bell rings, and I stand, my legs still wobbly. I take a deep breath, my heart still racing, and walk toward the door.  

*Maybe next time, I’ll be better.*  

*Maybe next time, I’ll be brave.*  

*Maybe next time, I’ll be… me.*
