**Item #: SCP-XXXX**  
**Object Class:** Euclid  

**Special Containment Procedures:**  
SCP-XXXX-1 (the unmarked doors) are to be monitored via global surveillance networks and field reports. All abandoned buildings with unexplained structural anomalies are to be surveyed by Mobile Task Force Mu-12 ("The Watchers") for potential SCP-XXXX-1 instances. No containment measures are feasible for SCP-XXXX-2 (the archive), as it exists outside conventional spatial and temporal parameters. Personnel entering SCP-XXXX-2 must be Level 4 clearance or higher, accompanied by at least two O5 Council members. All exploration logs and recovered documents must be submitted to Site-77 for analysis.  

**Description:**  
SCP-XXXX-1 consists of identical, unmarked doors measuring 2.1 meters in height and 1.2 meters in width. These doors are found in abandoned buildings worldwide, including derelict hospitals, collapsed bunkers, and forgotten subways. The doors are indistinguishable from standard architectural elements, lacking handles, hinges, or visible mechanisms. When opened, they lead to SCP-XXXX-2, a vast, architecturally impossible archive.  

SCP-XXXX-2 defies Euclidean geometry, with corridors that loop infinitely, staircases that ascend into voids, and rooms that exist simultaneously in multiple locations. The archive contains an estimated 1.2 × 10^18 documents, though no discernible pattern governs their arrangement. All documents are written in a variant of English, with some predating recorded history.  

Documents recovered from SCP-XXXX-2 reference a "previous global civilization" known as the "Era of the First Architects." This civilization, according to records, constructed SCP-XXXX-2 as a repository for knowledge, only to be destroyed by a "containment failure" that "unlocked the boundaries between what is and what was." The cause of this failure remains unspecified, though some texts suggest it involved the misuse of "architectural resonance," a concept later echoed in Foundation research.  

Foundation researchers have discovered references to their own organization within SCP-XXXX-2. A 1943 document titled *The Vault of Echoes* states:  
> "The Architects foresaw the rise of the Watchers. Their methods, though crude, mirror our own. The Vault is not a prison, but a mirror. Beware the day the mirror turns."  
Further analysis revealed personnel files for current Foundation staff, including Dr. ██████ and Agent █████, dated 212 BCE. These files describe the individuals' roles in "preparing the Archive for the Architects' return."  

**Addendum XXXX-1: Exploration Log 1**  
*Date: 20██-██-██*  
*Team: MTF Mu-12, Lead: Lt. █████*  

The team located SCP-XXXX-1 in a derelict subway station in █████, Czech Republic. The door was embedded in a wall with no visible mechanism. Upon opening, the team entered a corridor that extended infinitely in both directions. The air was thick with the scent of aged paper and ozone.  

"Look at this," said Agent █████, gesturing to a wall of documents. "These aren't just records—they're *instructions*."  

The team discovered a section of the archive labeled *Project Echo*. Documents described the previous civilization's attempt to "preserve knowledge across time" by embedding their records within a spatial anomaly. One document, dated 1234 CE, warned:  
> "The Vault is not a place. It is a *condition*. To enter is to become part of the Archive. The Architects did not destroy themselves—they *became* the Archive."  

The team encountered entities resembling hazmat teams, clad in white suits with respirators. These entities moved with mechanical precision, dismantling sections of the archive. When approached, they froze and fled, leaving behind fragments of documents.  

**Addendum XXXX-2: Exploration Log 2**  
*Date: 20██-██-██*  
*Team: MTF Mu-12, Lead: Lt. █████*  

The team returned to SCP-XXXX-2, now aware of the hazmat entities. They discovered a section of the archive being dismantled, with walls dissolving into "data streams" of text. One entity, later designated SCP-XXXX-3, was captured and interrogated.  

SCP-XXXX-3 spoke in fragmented sentences, repeating: "Containment. The Vault. The Architects." It was terminated via tranquilizer, but its final words were:  
> "They are coming. The Vault is *alive*."  

Further investigation revealed that SCP-XXXX-2 contains references to the O5 Council. A 1987 document titled *The Council's First Meeting* describes an O5 member named "Dr. █████" who "visited the Vault in 1943, before the Watchers were formed." This document was later confirmed to be a reference to O5-█, who was indeed alive in 1943.

**Addendum XXXX-4: O5 Council Note**  
*Date: 20██-██-██*  
*Author: O5-█*  

SCP-XXXX-2 is a *loop*. The previous civilization was the Foundation. The "containment failure" was the creation of SCP-XXXX-2 itself. The hazmat entities are remnants of the Foundation's past, tasked with maintaining the archive's stability. The O5 Council has visited SCP-XXXX-2 multiple times, including an unauthorized mission in 1943 led by O5-█. The mission's goal was to "prevent the Vault from becoming a reality."  

The Foundation's existence is a *repetition*. The Architects were the first Foundation, and the current Foundation is their echo. The Vault is not a threat—it is a *necessity*. To close it would erase all knowledge of the Foundation's origins.

*Date: 20██-██-██*

The O5 Council has authorized increased surveillance of SCP-XXXX-2, with the caveat that "the Vault must never be closed." The Foundation's role as a "mirror" is to ensure the Architects' return remains a possibility.
